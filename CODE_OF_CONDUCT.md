# Code of conduct

## Our commitment

Project participants must provide a professional, respectful, and harassment-free environment regardless of experience, identity, background, disability, appearance, nationality, ethnicity, religion, or other personal characteristics.

## Expected behavior

- Discuss technical ideas and evidence rather than attacking people.
- Give actionable, specific review feedback.
- Accept correction and update conclusions when tests or sources disagree.
- Respect security embargoes, privacy, and confidential reports.
- Avoid publishing credentials, customer data, private infrastructure, or other sensitive information.
- Make space for different levels of experience and explain project-specific terminology.
- Disclose relevant conflicts of interest.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, threats, discrimination, stalking, or unwanted sexual attention.
- Personal insults, sustained disruption, trolling, or deliberate humiliation.
- Publishing another person's private information without permission.
- Retaliation against reporters, reviewers, or maintainers acting in good faith.
- Deliberately bypassing security, compatibility, or integrity gates and concealing the change.
- Using project spaces to promote illegal access, malware, credential theft, or harm.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may edit or remove comments, reject contributions, temporarily restrict participation, or permanently ban participants whose conduct harms the community or project.

Report conduct concerns privately to the repository maintainers. Do not use a public issue when the report contains personal or security-sensitive information. Maintainers will limit disclosure to people needed to investigate and respond.

Enforcement decisions consider severity, intent, repeated behavior, harm, cooperation, and risk to affected people. Good-faith technical disagreement is not misconduct; abusive conduct framed as technical disagreement is.

## Scope

This policy applies in repository issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews, security reports, release coordination, and public spaces where someone represents the project.
